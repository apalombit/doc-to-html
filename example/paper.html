Frost Heave Under Lightweight Footbridges:
A Field Study of Three Timber Crossings

Maren Solberg1, Pieter Achterberg2 and Lucia Ferrante1

1Department of Civil Engineering, Fjordvik University, Norway

2Delta Structural Monitoring BV, Delft, the Netherlands

{m.solberg, l.ferrante}@fjordvik.example

Abstract—Short-span timber footbridges on shallow pad foundations are often built on frost-susceptible soils. We monitored three crossings in central Norway through two winters and found seasonal heave of 8 to 15 mm at the abutments, enough to open the deck joints and tilt the handrail posts. Heave tracked the square root of the frost index closely, and a simple coefficient fitted at one site predicted the other two within 2 mm.

I. Introduction

Most footbridges built by small municipalities are light timber structures with a single span of 15 to 35 m. Their foundations are chosen for cost, and where the ground freezes, pad footings above the frost line are common.1 The resulting movement is rarely measured, although it is a frequent cause of maintenance calls.

This paper reports two winters of monitoring at three such crossings and proposes a one-parameter model for the heave of their abutments.

II. Method

Each abutment carried two survey targets read weekly with a total station. Air temperature was logged hourly, and the frost index F was taken as the sum of daily mean temperatures below zero. We fitted the relation

Δh = α √F(1)

where Δh is the heave in millimetres and α a site coefficient.

1The national guidance permits this where the span is under 40 m and the deck is not continuous over the supports.
