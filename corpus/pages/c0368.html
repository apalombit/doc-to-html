Charge Recovery in Small Ember-Class Cells

Kayla Brennan, Brandon Shapiro, Malik Pruitt

Oakridge Institute of Materials, Boise, ID · University of Santa Fe, Santa Fe, NM · July 30, 2021

Abstract. We measure how much stored charge a small lithium cell regains during rest after a hard discharge. A simple relaxation model fits eleven cells to within 3% over six hours. We find that recovery depends more on temperature than on the depth of discharge, and we suggest a rest interval for field use.

1 Introduction

Small cells in sensors and handheld tools are often discharged hard and then left idle. Users report that the device works again after a pause, although the cell was nominally empty. This effect has been known for decades.

“A cell is never as empty as its gauge claims, nor as full after a rest as its owner hopes.”

Here we describe the recovery in terms of a single time constant and show how it can be estimated from a short test, without opening the cell.

2 Model

We write the recovered voltage after a rest of duration t as an exponential approach to its final value:

V(t) = V∞ − (V∞ − V0) e−t/τ(1)

where V0 is the voltage at the end of discharge, V∞ the rested voltage and τ the relaxation time. The recovered fraction follows as

η = (V∞ − V0) / V0(2)

3 Method

Eleven cells of 2,000 mAh were discharged at 2 C to 2.8 V, then rested at 5, 20 and 35 °C. Voltage was logged every ten seconds for six hours. Each run was repeated three times and the mean taken.

4 Results

At 20 °C the fitted τ averaged 41 minutes, with a spread of 6 minutes between cells. At 5 °C it rose to 96 minutes, and at 35 °C it fell to 23 minutes. The recovered fraction η varied little with the depth of discharge, between 0.062 and 0.071.

Residuals were below 3% for ten of the eleven cells. The eleventh, an older cell, showed a slow second tail that a single exponential does not capture.

5 Discussion

A rest of three time constants returns about 95% of the available recovery. In the field, that means roughly two hours in the cold and one hour at room temperature.
