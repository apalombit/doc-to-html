Tamarisk Honey Loaf

A dense, golden loaf from the Flagstaff test kitchen, sweetened only with honey.

This is the loaf we bake when the pantry is bare but the afternoon is long. Honey keeps the crumb moist for days, and a spoonful of ground tamarisk-style sumac, which tastes faintly of lemon, lifts the sweetness without sugar. It slices cleanly the next morning and toasts well.

Tested by Emily Abernathy and Maria Sullivan at 7,000 feet, where the oven runs hot and the dough rises fast; sea-level cooks should expect an extra five minutes.

MakesOne 9-inch loaf
Prep15 minutes
Bake55 minutes
Rest20 minutes
TestedMacon, GA, March 2022

Ingredients

  • 2½ cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • ¾ tsp fine salt
  • 1 tsp ground sumac
  • ¾ cup honey
  • ½ cup melted butter
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 cup buttermilk
  • ½ cup chopped walnuts

Method

  1. 1.Heat the oven to 350°F. Butter a 9 by 5-inch pan and line it with parchment.
  2. 2.Whisk the flour, baking soda, salt and sumac in a large bowl.
  3. 3.In a second bowl beat the honey, butter and eggs until smooth, then stir in the buttermilk.
  4. 4.Fold the wet mixture into the dry until just combined; fold in the walnuts. Do not overmix.
  5. 5.Pour into the pan and bake until a skewer comes out clean, about 55 minutes. Cool 20 minutes before turning out.
