What the ledgers show about Erie and Peoria

Harbor trade, wages and the long view, 1990 to 2020

When Aaliyah Shapiro began reading the freight ledgers of the Vale Terminal Company in the winter of 2023, she expected a story of steady loss. What she found was a pattern of plateaus: long periods in which little changed, broken by sudden moves in one or two years.

The numbers for Erie, PA bear this out. Tonnage handled at the lakefront rose through the 1990s, flattened, and then climbed again after 2015, when a regional grain contract was signed. Peoria, IL, on the Illinois River, followed a related but slower course.

Reading the index

The index in Figure 4.1 sets the 1990 volume of each town at 38 and scales later years to the same base. It is not a count of ships, and it should not be read as one. It is a measure of how much of a town's working day was spent moving goods.

Three features stand out. First, the 2010 dip is real and appears in every ledger, though not equally. Second, wages followed volume with a lag of about two years. Third, the later gains were made with fewer people, a point to which the next chapter returns.

A note on method

Each entry was entered twice by separate readers and compared. Where the two entries differed, a third reading settled the matter. Sofia Brennan checked a sample of one in twenty against the originals held in the county archive.

Darnell Mahoney, who worked on the docks until 2009, supplied the account of how a day's tally was written up, which explains the odd habit of rounding to the nearest ten.
