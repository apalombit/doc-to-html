Chapter 4

Cities on the water

When a lake town stops depending on its harbor for freight, its shoreline is left with a question: who is it for? Erie, Pennsylvania, answered by opening its bayfront to walkers, anglers and small boats, while keeping a working dock at the western end.

Planners describe the change in three steps. First the old industrial frontage is cleared and tested for contamination. Next, public access is built, usually a promenade with benches and lighting. Last, private investment follows the foot traffic.

Greenville, South Carolina, took a different road. Its river runs through the center of town, and the city rebuilt the banks around a park rather than a harbor. Visitors there count in the millions a year, and the downtown shops that once faced away from the water now open onto it. Students should compare the two cases against the three steps above.
