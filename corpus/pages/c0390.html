Chapter 6

The Salisbury Plain Carriers

By the autumn of 2019 the carriers who worked the road from Salisbury to the coast were moving a third more cargo than a decade before, and doing it with fewer wagons. Priya Llewellyn, who kept the ledgers for one of the larger firms, recalled that the change came not from any single invention but from a hundred small economies.

What changed

  • Loads were weighed before departure
    • Overloaded axles were refused
    • Empty return trips fell sharply
  • Routes were fixed by the season
    • Winter runs kept to the main road
  • Stabling was shared between firms

Amelia Kaur, writing from Inverness, noted that the northern carriers followed much the same course, though some years behind. The Lodestar company, she observed, copied the Salisbury timetables almost line for line.

The figures bear out what the carriers said of themselves. Fewer wagons, heavier loads and steadier timetables together made the road pay in years when the harbour trade was slack, and the habit of sharing stables lasted well beyond the period studied here.
