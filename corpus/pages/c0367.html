1. Introduction

Narrow firths mix their water differently from open coasts. In the survey described here, eleven moorings recorded temperature and salinity through twelve tidal cycles, and the vertical difference in density, Δρ, was found to fall below 0.4 kg m−3 during every spring ebb.1

We argue that the rate of mixing scales with the cube of the tidal current, in line with earlier work, and that a stratified layer re-forms within about six hours of slack water.2

2. Method

Each mooring carried a conductivity sensor at 4 m and 18 m. Records were averaged over ten minutes, and gaps shorter than half an hour were filled by linear interpolation.

1 Spring ebb: the outgoing tide at new or full moon.
2 Slack water: the short period when the tidal current is near zero.
