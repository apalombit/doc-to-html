5   Results

5.1   Main Results

For each cell, we compute RCG and OSR for 50
random draws of 100 human respondents. We first
average the scores over draws within each cell, then
average the resulting cell-level scores across cells.
We also confirm that RCG behaves as intended;
in each cell c,9 we replace the generated set Gc
with a separate sample of 100 human respondents
drawn from Hc, disjoint from the 100 respondents
used for evaluation, and compute RCG exactly as
above. The average RCG is 1.001 (WVS) and
1.000 (ANES). The same replacement gives an av-
erage OSR of 1.001 (WVS) and 1.002 (ANES).
Results under the hold-out fit setting are reported
in Appendix I.

5.2   Failure Modes

Using the uncalibrated OSR column of Table 1,
we next examine how each LLM fails to cover
the human respondents. The baselines (Marginals,
Copula, and LCA) have OSR at or near one (0.946–
1.078) on both datasets. RCG alone does not show
how a model fails: on ANES, the six LLMs span
1.277–1.370 in RCG but 0.838–1.213 in OSR.
In both datasets, OSR splits the six LLMs into
two groups: the upper group (gpt-oss-20b, gpt-
5.4-nano, and Qwen3.5-9B) and the lower group
(gemma-4-12B, gemini-flash-lite, and Llama-3.3-
70B). The OSRs of the lower group are below one
(0.722–0.840 on WVS and 0.838–0.942 on ANES)
and the RCGs are above one. By the definitions in
Section 3.2, this combination indicates contraction.
In the upper group, the OSRs on ANES are above
one (1.144–1.213), which is the off-support pattern.
By contrast, on WVS, the OSRs are close to one
(0.992–1.013).
