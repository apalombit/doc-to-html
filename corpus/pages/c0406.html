5.3   Results

Table 2 reports empirical miscoverage, RMSE between the median predicted trajectory and the
observed weekly cases, and average pointwise interval length for the two proposed constructions
and the MCMC baseline. Figure 6 displays the proposed bands overlaid on the Malawi data,
with miscovered weeks highlighted in orange; the KOH–MCMC band was shown in Figure 1a.

Both proposed parameter sets achieve an empirical miscoverage of 0.1379, moderately above
the target α = 0.1 and consistent with the CRC guarantee 𝔼[Ln+1(λ̂)] ≤ α, with average
interval lengths of 2540.26 and 3157.48 cases. The MCMC baseline attains a miscoverage of
0.0345, less than half the nominal level, with the widest band (3526.89 cases); this pattern is
consistent with the sensitivity of posterior uncertainty to the assumed discrepancy specification
(Brynjarsdóttir and O’Hagan, 2014, Syring and Martin, 2019). The CRC procedure instead
calibrates λ̂ directly against the empirical miscoverage of synthetic data generated under the
heterogeneous GP family, sidestepping the need for any single parametric noise model to be
exactly correct. The RMSE values of the three methods are comparable. The ablation study
in Section S4 of the Supplementary Material isolates the role of the CRC step on these data:
retaining a fixed 90% of the generated draws without CRC, under either selection rule, attains
zero miscoverage on the evaluated weeks but produces bands considerably wider than both the
CRC and KOH–MCMC bands, indicating overcoverage (cf. Figure 1b).
