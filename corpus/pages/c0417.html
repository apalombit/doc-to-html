6.2Sampling a watershed

Learning objectives
  1. 1.Describe how a sampling plan is designed for a stream reach.
    1. (a)Choose sites above and below each suspected source.
    2. (b)Fix the sampling interval before the first visit.
  2. 2.Explain why replicate samples are taken at every site.
  3. 3.Calculate a stream’s discharge from its width, depth and velocity.
    1. (a)Measure depth at five points across the channel.
    2. (b)Time a float over a marked 10 m run.

Discharge is the volume of water passing a cross-section each second. For a channel of roughly rectangular section it is the product of three measurements:

Q = w × d × v (6.1)
Worked example 6.2. On April 17, 2022, Jordan Pruitt’s field class measured Ochre Creek near Macon, GA. Jasmine Brennan found w = 4.2 m and Aaliyah Brandt found a mean depth d = 0.35 m; the float time gave v = 0.6 m/s. So Q = 4.2 × 0.35 × 0.6 = 0.88 m3/s.
Check your understanding
A second class at a creek near Sioux Falls, SD, measured w = 3.0 m, d = 0.5 m and v = 0.4 m/s. Is its discharge larger or smaller than Ochre Creek’s?
