Sorrel and Rye Sourdough Boule

A dark, tangy loaf with a lemony green edge, for the cold weeks before the first frost.

Recipe by Mei AdeyemiTested in Tulsa, OKMakes 1 loaf, 12 slicesActive 40 min, total 22 hr

Why it works

Rye gives the crumb its earthy depth, and a fistful of wild sorrel stirred in at the last fold adds a bright sourness that butter loves. Aaliyah Hendricks, who first baked it for a church supper in Ithaca, NY, keeps the dough wetter than you expect. Resist the urge to add flour.

hydration = water ÷ flour × 100 = 360 ÷ 500 × 100 = 72%

Keep the arithmetic in mind when you change the flours: whole grains drink more, so a loaf with 30% rye wants a few extra spoons of water.

Sorrel wilts quickly. Chop it just before folding it in, or it will bleed green through the whole dough.

Method

  1. 1. The night before, mix the flours, water and starter until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest for 40 minutes.
  2. 2. Sprinkle over the salt and caraway, wet your hand, and pinch it in. Fold the dough over itself in the bowl eight times.
  3. 3. Over the next two hours, fold three more times, working in the sorrel on the last fold. Leave the dough somewhere cool overnight, about 12 hours.
  4. 4. Turn the dough onto a floured counter, shape it into a tight round, and set it seam side up in a floured basket. Rest 90 minutes.
  5. 5. Heat a covered cast-iron pot in the oven at 475°F for 45 minutes. Tip the loaf in, score it once, and bake covered for 25 minutes.
  6. 6. Uncover and bake 18 minutes more, until the crust is deep brown. Cool on a rack for at least one hour before cutting.

To serve

Sofia Abernathy slices it thin for open sandwiches with smoked trout and horseradish. It keeps three days wrapped in a cloth, and toasts well on the fourth.
