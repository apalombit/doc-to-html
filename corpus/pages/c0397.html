2 Methods and model

2.2 Single-pool litter decay

We describe the dry mass of litter remaining in a bag at time t by a single-pool exponential, where m0 is the initial dry mass and k the decay constant in yr−1. Following the bag design used on the Vale transect near Durham, each of the 240 bags held 5.00 g of air-dried material from one of four species.

(1)m(t) = m0 e−kt

The half-life follows directly as t½ = ln 2 / k. Bags were buried at 5 cm and recovered after 3, 6, 12, 18 and 24 months; recovered material was cleaned of roots, dried at 60 °C for 48 h and weighed to 0.01 g.

2.3 Temperature dependence

The constant was allowed to vary with soil temperature T (°C) through a Q10 relationship anchored at a reference temperature Tref of 10 °C, so that a rise of ten degrees multiplies the rate by a factor of Q10, as equation (2) states.

(2)k(T) = kref · Q10(T − Tref)/10

Hourly temperatures came from one logger per plot, placed at bag depth, and were averaged over each burial interval before fitting. Where a logger failed, which happened for 11 of the 48 plots, the mean of the two nearest plots was used.1

2.4 Estimation

Parameters were estimated for each species by non-linear least squares, minimising the residual sum of squares over all bags of that species,

(3)S = Σi ( mi − m̂i )2

where mi is the observed and m̂i the fitted mass of bag i. Standard errors were taken from the inverse Hessian at the optimum and checked against 2000 bootstrap resamples drawn by plot rather than by bag, since bags in one plot share a soil and a logger.

Model comparison used the corrected Akaike criterion, AICc = 2p − 2 ln L + 2p(p + 1)/(n − p − 1), with p parameters and n bags. A two-pool alternative was rejected for every species except bracken, where the Fern Hill plots showed a fast early loss of about 12% in the first three months (Kaur and MacLeod, 2017).

All fits were made in R 3.5 using code archived with the data; the Colchester field laboratory held the original weighing sheets.2
