Eleven days on the Pacific Crest, one sock at a time
By Tomasz Brennan · Photographs by Lena Aldous

The trail begins at a dusty monument and ends, if you are lucky, at a lake you have been promising yourself since August. In between lie 212 miles of switchbacks, snowmelt creeks and the particular loneliness of a campsite at 9,400 feet. Brennan set out on October 2 with a 22-pound pack and a plan that lasted until the second afternoon.

What follows is a practical account: what he carried, what he regretted, and the eleven small decisions that made the difference between a hard week and a memorable one. Water, as ever, decided everything. Resupply, in the end, decided the rest of it.

Along the way he met a retired schoolteacher walking south in sandals, two trail crews rebuilding a washed-out bridge, and a ranger who asked, kindly, whether he had considered a lighter stove. He had. He had also considered quitting at mile 87, and again at mile 140, and each time the next lake changed his mind. Plan on 18 miles a day, carry a spare pair of socks, and never trust a creek marked seasonal on the map.

“You do not conquer a ridge. You are tolerated on it.”
