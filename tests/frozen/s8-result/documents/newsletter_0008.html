RIVERSIDE

Boathouse reach to reopen after eighteen months of repair

BY ADELA FENN, LOCAL GOVERNMENT CORRESPONDENT

The stretch of towpath between Bridge Street

and the old lock will reopen to the public on the first of October, the borough council confirmed on Tuesday, bringing to an end a closure that has lasted rather longer than anyone intended.

Work began in March of last year after a survey found that the retaining wall had moved by nearly forty millimetres. What was scheduled as a nine-month job ran into the winter floods, and then into a dispute over who owned the culvert beneath the eastern end.

The final cost is put at £1.42 million, against an original estimate of £860,000. Councilor Ruth Amberley, who chairs the environment committee, described the overrun as regrettable but unavoidable.

We found three problems the survey could not have seen, and each one had to be solved before the next became visible.

CLLR RUTH AMBERLEY

WHAT REOPENS, AND WHEN

The towpath itself opens on the first. The two slipways follow a fortnight later, once the new handrails have been tested. The boathouse café, closed since the scaffolding went up, expects to trade again before the half-term holiday.

Castleton Rowing Club, which has trained from a temporary landing stage upstream throughout, will move its equipment back over the last weekend of the month. The club's captain said the arrangement had cost it perhaps a dozen members.

THE CULVERT QUESTION

The dispute that added four months to the schedule concerned a brick culvert running under the path near the lock. Neither the council nor the navigation authority had it on a drawing, and neither wished to pay to rebuild it.

It was settled in June by a joint agreement under which the cost is shared in the same proportion as the two bodies' frontage. The culvert has been relined rather than replaced, which is expected to give it another forty years.

A commemorative plaque, paid for by public subscription, will be unveiled at eleven o'clock on the morning of the reopening. The mayor will attend. There will be tea.
