Abstract—Short-span timber footbridges on shallow pad foundations are often built on frost-susceptible soils. We monitored three crossings in central Norway through two winters and found seasonal heave of 8 to 15 mm at the abutments, enough to open the deck joints and tilt the handrail posts. Heave tracked the square root of the frost index closely, and a simple coefficient fitted at one site predicted the other two within 2 mm.

I. Introduction

Most footbridges built by small municipalities are light timber structures with a single span of 15 to 35 m. Their foundations are chosen for cost, and where the ground freezes, pad footings above the frost line are common.¹ The resulting movement is rarely measured, although it is a frequent cause of maintenance calls.

This paper reports two winters of monitoring at three such crossings and

proposes a one-parameter model for the heave of their abutments.

II. METHOD

Each abutment carried two survey targets read weekly with a total station. Air temperature was logged hourly, and the frost index F was taken as the sum of daily mean temperatures below zero. We fitted the relation
